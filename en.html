Chanote — the highest form of land title in Thailand: a perpetual right to the house and land with precisely surveyed boundaries, no term and no lease. How that right is registered to you — we'll arrange a lawful structure for your situation and walk the deal through with your lawyer.

Straight answers to what actually decides the deal

Can a foreigner buy and own this villa?

Yes. Foreign buyers regularly purchase villas in Phuket. We'll go through the optimal ownership structure for your situation individually — and we welcome legal review on your side.

What's already included — and what you won't need to buy?

Everything, turnkey. Furniture, all appliances, a fully equipped kitchen, linens — arrive with a suitcase and live from the first evening, nothing to buy. We hand over the full furniture and appliance inventory and lock it into the contract, so you know exactly what you're getting.

How quickly can I view the villa — in person or online?

Message us on WhatsApp — we'll arrange an in-person viewing within 1–2 days. If you're not in Phuket, we'll do a detailed video tour on a call.

Is the title clean — what can I check before a deposit?

Yes: Chanote land title, no mortgages or encumbrances. Before any deposit we hand the full package to your lawyer — the title, plot boundaries and building permit can all be checked in advance.

How realistic is renting it out, and what income to expect?

Yes — and the villa already rents. Rawai and Nai Harn see year-round demand: nightly rentals from tourists and long-term leases from expats living in Phuket. On full rental, occupancy reaches 80–90% a year and the yield is around 8–10% net on the price. The documented figures for the past period are lower: for several months the owner lived in the villa. With a stronger management company or self-rental it can go higher still. Model it on your own assumptions in the calculator above; actual contracts and figures shared at the meeting.

Why are you selling the villa?

The owner is relocating to another country. The villa is well kept, in full order and already earning — the sale has nothing to do with the condition of the house or the documents.

What's the net yield after all costs?

As a guide, 8–10% net on the price on full rental (80–90% occupancy) — already after running and management costs (≈46% of gross). The exact figure depends on the season and the management company — it's a guide, not a guarantee; actual contracts and figures are shared at the meeting.
